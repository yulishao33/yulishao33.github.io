Emotional Design &
Playfulness in Learning

I built a screen-based interactive lesson on lunar phases to investigate how playful design features and emotional states interact with cognitive processes to produce learning outcomes.

P

Research Problem

Emotional design research shows mixed results sometimes it works, sometimes it doesn't. Why? For whom? Through what mechanisms do emotion, motivation, and behavioral engagement connect to learning outcomes? These are the questions at the heart of my dissertation.

T

Thought Process

Moving from VR to screen-based learning, I had to rethink media affordances entirely. What does a web-based interactive lesson offer that VR doesn't and how do multimedia principles around spatial layout, modality, and cognitive load apply differently? My specialty in emotional design shaped every design decision.

M

Methods & Tools

Factorial experimental design (A/B testing) to isolate the effect of playful UI features. Think-aloud protocols to capture real-time emotional states; self-reports alone miss in-the-moment fluctuation. Log data analysis and latent profile analysis to identify distinct learner engagement patterns. Survey instruments built in Qualtrics. Statistical analysis in R and SPSS.

Experimental Design Think-Aloud Log Data Analysis Latent Profile Analysis R / SPSS Figma NGSS-Aligned Instructional Design
People do find certain things more playful but the link between affect and learning outcomes is shaped by prior interest and individual tendencies. It's delicate. That's precisely why it matters.
Dissertation finding

Key Finding

Playful design features (micro-interactions, game-like sound effects) do shift emotional states but their effect on learning is bounded by learner characteristics: interest, prior knowledge, and trait playfulness. The boundary conditions are the insight.

Outcomes
✓ 3 engagement profiles identified via LPA ✓ Boundary conditions of emotional design mapped ✓ Dissertation defense: Spring 2025

VR Design &
Research

A VR learning simulation for high school students to explore lunar phases designed from scratch using needs assessment, co-design workshops, and learning science theory.

P

Research Problem

Students consistently struggle to understand spatial relationships between celestial bodies. Traditional 2D instruction fails to build the mental models needed, leaving persistent misconceptions about lunar phases.

T

Thought Process

VR's unique affordances (presence, embodiment, multiple perspectives) offered something 2D couldn't: letting students physically inhabit the solar system. Research on presence and affective states confirmed this could enhance motivation and situational interest, priming better learning.

M

Methods & Tools

Stakeholder interviews with NYC teachers, museum educators, and physics experts. Literature review on spatial cognition and multimedia learning principles. Competitor analysis of existing simulations. Iterative co-design workshops with public school students and teachers. Usability testing with hundreds of NYC high schoolers.

Needs Assessment Co-design Usability Testing Figma Multimedia Principles

Design Insight

It doesn't make sense to port a 2D lesson into VR unchanged. We designed explicitly for what VR enables: traveling from Earth to the Moon, observing phases from multiple vantage points, and testing spatial hypotheses that would be impossible in a classroom.

Does the Earth go through phases when you observe it from the Moon? The kind of transfer question only VR makes possible to test.
Outcomes
✓ Testified by teachers and students at NYU and partner schools ✓ Hundreds of students reached ✓ Book chapter on VR affordances published ✓ Ongoing generative AI tutor integration

My Role

Project Lead. Responsible for research design, stakeholder engagement, learning experience design, experimental study design and data collection, research dissemination, and cross-functional coordination between researchers, developers, and school partners.

Games for
Wellbeing

A two-phase international study (UNICEF and Lego Foundation) investigating whether and how digital gameplay can positively impact children's wellbeing across the US, Chile, and South Africa.

P

Research Problem

Public opinion on kids and video games skews negative. But what does the evidence actually say? This project asked: can casual digital gameplay meaningfully support child wellbeing, and if so, through which dimensions? The wellbeing framework (grounded in self-determination theory) defined six core constructs: competency, agency/autonomy, relatedness, curiosity, optimism, and relaxation.

T

Thought Process

No existing framework existed for selecting games based on wellbeing potential. I built one from scratch: a mixed-methods game analysis protocol that coded in-game events against six wellbeing dimensions, triangulated with children's think-aloud data and researcher observations. This produced a "wellbeing profile" for each game, making selection decisions rigorous and transparent rather than arbitrary.

M

Methods and Tools

Phase 1: Lab sessions with 20 children ages 8 to 12. Video analysis with a custom coding scheme matching in-game events to wellbeing dimensions. Three data sources triangulated: video coding, child interviews, researcher observations. Final game wellbeing profiles produced for titles including Plants vs. Zombies, Angry Birds, Lego Builder's Journey, and Lego Tower.

Phase 2: 10-week field experiment across three countries. Weekly visits to afterschool programs. Observation logs, surveys, post-interviews, and social network analysis to map how gameplay shaped peer connections over time.

Mixed Methods Video Analysis Think-Aloud Social Network Analysis Experimental Design Cross-Cultural Research

Phase 1 Output: Wellbeing Game Profiles

Each game was rated against all six wellbeing dimensions using a multi-source coding system. For example, Lego Builder's Journey scored high on relatedness: children narrated the separated characters aloud, expressing belonging through the game's story. These profiles gave the team an evidence-based, actionable matrix for game selection.

The games were single-player, but the kids made them social. They shared tactics, challenged each other, built narratives together. The game became the bridge.
Field observation, Phase 2

Cross-Cultural Nuance

Social network analysis showed distinct cultural patterns: US children formed one large cluster with no clear subgroups; Chilean and South African children played in tighter, more defined smaller groups. The same games, very different social dynamics. This intercultural layer added meaningful depth to the findings.

Outcomes
✓ Novel game wellbeing profiling framework ✓ Evidence of wellbeing benefits (esp. belongingness) ✓ Study replicated in Chile and South Africa ✓ Publications contributed as research lead ✓ Framework transferable to game design practice

GitHub Enterprise:
AI Governance Research

Foundational UX research into how enterprise customers govern AI access and actions, and a novel solution for making research findings actually learnable across a cross-functional team.

P

Research Problem

Enterprise customers using GitHub Copilot lacked the guardrails to govern AI at scale. What governance frameworks do they need? What are their pain points around access control, auditing, and accountability? This was an uncharted design space requiring foundational discovery research.

T

Thought Process

I approached this as a two-track problem: understanding customers, and making sure that understanding actually landed with the right people inside GitHub. Before talking to any customer, I spent the first two weeks interviewing 8 internal stakeholders to map what was already known, what decisions were blocked, and what success looked like for each team. This grounded the research plan in real product needs. Throughout data collection, I updated stakeholders after each session with notes, transcripts, and key takeaways so findings accumulated visibly in real time rather than arriving all at once at the end.

M

Methods & Tools

Multi-channel recruitment via Customer Success Managers, Customer Advisory Board, GitHub Stars, and screener surveys, resulting in 10+ enterprise accounts interviewed within 2 weeks. Semi-structured customer interviews with iterative guide refinement. Parallel stakeholder engagement across PMs, designers, and Customer Success Managers to triangulate findings and contextualize insights. Synthesis sessions to co-develop research directions before final reporting. Outputs delivered as report, slides, and a GitHub Spark-built interactive web report with role-based navigation.

Discovery Research Stakeholder Interviews Foundational Research Multi-Channel Recruitment GitHub Spark Research Communication
Two distinct enterprise admin personas emerged: the Guardian, focused on security, AI compliance, and legal risk mitigation; and the Enabler, focused on fast AI adoption without losing organizational control. By mapping company characteristics (size, industry regulation, and AI maturity) to these personas, I built a decision matrix that directly informed GitHub's enterprise feature roadmap.
Key research finding

The Interactive Report Innovation

Rather than a static PDF, I prototyped an interactive web report as a personal initiative using GitHub Spark. Stakeholders can self-identify their role and explore findings via their own path, with features like quote banks filterable by topic and company profiles mapped intuitively to personas. Early feedback from PMs and leadership at the share-out pointed to strong promise, and it opened conversations about rethinking how research outputs are delivered at GitHub.

Outcomes
✓ Identified Guardian and Enabler personas that grounded both roadmap prioritization and specific design decisions ✓ Built foundational understanding of enterprise AI governance, giving design teams evidence to answer "what would an admin do or feel?" ✓ Prototyped an interactive research report with role-based navigation and quote filtering, increasing visibility of research work and enabling faster adoption of findings ✓ Research bridged customer success, product, and design teams

Design

AI-Powered
Inquiry Learning

Designing an AI-powered platform to scaffold inquiry-based learning in STEM classrooms starting with physics, built with teachers and students across New York, India, and Germany.

P

Design Problem

Inquiry-based learning is powerful but hard to do well at scale. Students struggle to formulate testable hypotheses. Teachers lack tools to give personalized feedback to every student simultaneously. Data collection in experiments is slow, error-prone, and disconnected from deeper thinking.

T

Thought Process

Rather than letting AI replace student thinking, I designed a framework where AI scaffolds the inquiry process: prompting deeper questions, detecting data errors, offering adaptive hints based on prior knowledge. The goal is augmenting student agency, not replacing it, grounded in adaptive learning theory and evidence-based personalization frameworks.

M

Methods & Tools

Multi-stakeholder discovery interviews with NYC, Indian, and German teachers. Four-layer design framework: real-life pain points → scholarly evidence → AI capability boundaries → design space. Feature mapping to inquiry stages. Conceptualizing and designing evidence-based AI-supported scaffolds grounded in inquiry learning theory. MVP evaluation framework with ongoing formative iteration.

Stakeholder Research Design Framework Formative Evaluation AI-Augmented Design Rapid Prototyping Figma

Design Framework (4 Layers)

1. Real-life classroom pain points from teachers globally
2. Scholarly evidence on inquiry learning challenges
3. AI capability boundaries and technical constraints
4. Feature design space mapped to inquiry stages

AI shouldn't take ownership of students' learning. It should help them be more curious.
Outcomes
✓ MVP in active development ✓ Teacher partners across 3 countries ✓ Evaluation framework established

My Role

Project Lead. Responsible for needs assessment and stakeholder engagement across the US, India, and Germany, multi-layered research framework development, learning experience design, mixed-method research design with multimodal data, and cross-functional coordination between researchers, developers, and school partners.

AI &
Analytics Projects

Three focused research projects spanning systematic review methodology, process mining and think-aloud analysis, and NLP-driven predictive modeling, each tackling a distinct analytical challenge.

A

Process Mining: How Students Use ChatGPT for Data Analysis

Problem: Do students with lower prior knowledge benefit differently from AI assistance than experts? What strategies do they use while working through problems with AI support?

Method: 40+ students given data analysis tasks using ChatGPT. Think-aloud protocols coded for strategy type (passive prompting, active verification, challenge-and-check). Process mining to map learner-AI interaction patterns.

Process Mining Think-Aloud Coding ChatGPT Study AI Equity
B

Predictive Modeling: Early Academic Risk from Reflection Notes

Problem: Can a student's routine reflection notes signal academic risk before a crisis point? Dental school students submitted regular reflections; the goal was to help instructors intervene earlier.

Method: NLP pipeline parsing reflection texts into linguistic features (locus of control signals, frustration indicators, motivational language). Random forest model predicting academic risk probability at time of submission.

NLP Random Forest Text Analytics Learning Analytics R
C

Systematic Review: Generative AI and Student Learning Outcomes

Problem: What is the overall landscape of AI-in-education research? Does AI benefit or hinder student learning outcomes, and through what mechanisms?

Method: PRISMA framework. Comprehensive search across six databases (Web of Science, ERIC, Scopus, and others). Independently screened 472 articles with two co-authors. Thematic coding and synthesis.

PRISMA Systematic Review In Publication

Key Finding: Project A

Lower-knowledge students relied more directly on AI, while higher-knowledge students verified answers with external tools. Despite different strategies, both groups reached similar performance levels, suggesting AI has genuine potential to democratize learning outcomes and reduce knowledge-based inequality.

Key Finding: Project B

Transformed routine, unstructured student writing into an actionable early-warning signal. The model gives instructors a first-look indicator from the text itself, enabling earlier, more targeted support rather than waiting for grades or formal assessments to surface a problem.

Key Contribution: Project C

AI tools show substantial promise for learning outcomes, from broad academic performance to specific skills. Persistent field-level gaps remain: small sample sizes, lack of longitudinal studies, and insufficient attention to diverse learner populations. AI should complement, not replace, teachers.

AI has the potential to democratize education, putting students who don't have a lot of resources in a position to achieve at the same level as those who do.

International
Evaluation

Independent evaluation of five international scholarship programs including Fulbright and Gilman, serving thousands of scholars and culminating in a 20-year impact report on international exchange.

P

Research Problem

International scholarship programs have complex, multi-stakeholder evaluation needs. Donors, program managers, and scholars all care about different things. The challenge: design evaluations that are methodologically rigorous, contextually sensitive (including scholars in emergency situations), and deliver findings in formats that stakeholders can actually act on.

T

Thought Process

I approached evaluation as a communication problem, not just an analysis problem. Different donors had different levels of data literacy and different program missions. Producing one standard report and handing it over wouldn't serve anyone well. Instead, I engaged program managers and donors throughout the process, adapted output formats to audience, and ran participatory data workshops so stakeholders could find their own insights rather than passively receive mine.

M

Methods and Tools

Large-scale international survey design and analysis. Descriptive and inferential statistics across thousands of respondents. In-depth scholar interviews. Participatory data workshops with stakeholders. Multiple output formats: evaluation reports, data dashboards, and narrative-based workshops designed to match how different audiences process information.

Survey Design Large-Scale Data Analysis Stakeholder Interviews SPSS Data Workshops
A 20-year impact report covering 20,000+ scholars: not just a data product, but a narrative of how international exchange shapes people's lives across cultures and careers.
Gilman 20-Year Impact Report

Flagship Output: Gilman 20-Year Impact Report

Surveyed over 20,000 scholars and conducted 10 in-depth narrative interviews to document what a generation of international exchange had built. The report combined quantitative breadth with rich qualitative storytelling, designed to resonate with donors, policymakers, and the public alike.

Participatory Data Workshops

Rather than delivering evaluation reports for stakeholders to read alone, I facilitated structured data workshops where program managers engaged with findings actively. The workshop design drew on narrative learning principles: starting from stakeholders' own goals, building toward insight, and anchoring conclusions in real program context.

Outcomes
✓ 5 scholarship programs evaluated independently ✓ 20,000+ scholars surveyed (Gilman report) ✓ 10 in-depth scholar narratives produced ✓ Stakeholder workshops driving program improvement
